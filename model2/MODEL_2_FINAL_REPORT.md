## 1. Executive Summary

As electrical grids integrate higher shares of non-dispatchable photovoltaic (PV) generation, sudden supply-demand mismatches threaten grid voltage stability and escalate balancing reserve costs. This report details the design, implementation, and rigorous empirical validation of **Model 2: Solar Generation Forecasting**, which predicts the power output of 42 campus rooftop solar arrays 15 minutes into the future ($t+1$).

From an original corpus of 16 multi-domain CSV files, an exhaustive audit isolated the 5 files governing solar generation and atmospheric physics. Using an astronomically guided data cleaning strategy, continuous 15-minute time series were assembled across 2.46 million supervised instances spanning 2.31 years. Models were evaluated using a strict chronological split (70% Train, 15% Validation, 15% Test).

The finalized **XGBoost regressor** achieves an overall test $R^2$ of **0.9522** and a Daylight $R^2$ of **0.9432**, reducing daytime Mean Absolute Error (MAE) by **40.49%** ($1.228\text{ vs }2.064\text{ kWh}$) and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) by **38.59%** ($2.846\text{ vs }4.634\text{ kWh}$) over the standard industry persistence baseline. An extensive ablation study proves that while short-term autoregressive generation lags provide the foundational signal, injecting satellite irradiance and astronomical solar geometry provides essential incremental error reduction during cloudy, volatile weather.

## 2. Problem Definition & Mathematical Formulation

The forecasting objective is single-step ahead prediction of energy generation across distributed PV arrays:

$$\hat{y}_{i, t+1} = f(\mathbf{x}_{i, t})$$

Where:
- $\hat{y}_{i, t+1}$ is the predicted electrical energy generated by solar site $i$ during the 15-minute interval $(t, t+1]$, measured in **Kilowatt-hours ($\text{kWh}$)**.
- $\mathbf{x}_{i, t}$ is the feature vector strictly containing observations recorded at or before time $t$.
- **Forecast Horizon**: Exactly 15 minutes ahead.
- **Sampling Frequency**: Regular 15-minute intervals (96 intervals per 24-hour day).
- **Physical Boundedness**: Photovoltaic generation is non-negative ($\hat{y} \ge 0$) and physically bounded by solar irradiance and array nameplate capacity.

## 4. Data Cleaning, Alignment & Quality Assurance

### A. Astronomical Nighttime Imputation vs. Daytime Outages
The raw generation target contained 1,536,301 missing values (56.23%). Rather than blindly imputing zeros, solar position equations (NOAA Solar Position Algorithm) were computed for every timestamp:
- **Nighttime Intervals ($\text{solar elevation } \alpha \le 0^\circ$)**: Exactly $1,350,394$ missing values ($87.90\%$ of all missingness) occurred when the sun was below the geometric horizon. Solar loggers shut off below minimum startup thresholds and record nulls. These were legitimately imputed as **$0.00\text{ kWh}$**.
- **Daylight Outages ($\alpha > 0^\circ$)**: Exactly $185,907$ missing values ($12.10\%$ of all missingness) occurred during daylight, concentrated in multi-day consecutive blocks (e.g. Site 7 inverter failure in 2021). These represent hardware/communication outages and were **preserved as NaN**, excluding them from training loss to prevent training the model to predict 0 output under bright sunshine.

### B. Timezone Synchronization
A severe time misalignment was discovered: `Solar_Irradiance.csv` was recorded in UTC, while generation and weather were recorded in local Eastern Australian Time (`Australia/Melbourne`). Naive joining yielded a near-zero correlation ($r = 0.02$). Converting UTC to `Australia/Melbourne` increased irradiance-generation correlation to **$r = 0.8340$** ($r = 0.9103$ in winter).

### C. Cardinality and Alignment
Merging generation with site metadata and 15-minute weather/irradiance produced an exact 1:1 join across all **2,731,946 rows** with 0 duplicate keys and 0 row multiplication.

## 5. Leakage-Safe Feature Engineering

A candidate set of **33 features** was engineered strictly from past information:

1. **Autoregressive Lags**:
   - $lag_1$: Generation at time $t$ (immediate persistence)
   - $lag_2, lag_4$: 30-min and 60-min past generation (short-term momentum)
   - $lag_{96}$: Generation at the exact same 15-minute interval yesterday (24-hour seasonal lag)
   - $lag_{672}$: Generation at the exact same 15-minute interval 7 days ago (weekly cycle)
2. **Trailing Rolling Statistics (computed strictly on $lag_1$)**:
   - 1-hour trailing mean, std, max, min (captures trend, volatility/cloud intermittency, and peak envelope)
   - 4-hour trailing mean (morning/afternoon ramp tracking)
   - 24-hour trailing mean (multi-day weather regime indicator)
3. **Solar Geometry & Physical Models**:
   - Astronomical solar elevation ($\alpha$) and solar azimuth angle
   - Theoretical clear-sky Global Horizontal Irradiance ($\text{GHI}_\text{clear}$) calculated via the Haurwitz radiation model
4. **Satellite & Surface Meteorology**:
   - Satellite $\text{GHI}$ and Cloud Opacity (forward-filled from most recent scan)
   - Ambient air temperature, relative humidity, wind speed
   - Circular sine and cosine transformations of wind direction ($\sin\theta, \cos\theta$)
5. **Temporal & Spatial Encodings**:
   - Continuous diurnal sine/cosine clock encodings ($\sin(2\pi \cdot \text{hour}/24), \cos(2\pi \cdot \text{hour}/24)$)
   - Annual day-of-year cyclical encodings ($\sin(2\pi \cdot \text{doy}/365.25), \cos(2\pi \cdot \text{doy}/365.25)$)
   - Day-of-week and weekend indicators
   - Array nameplate capacity ($kW_p$), latitude, longitude, and categorical site identifiers

## 6. Experimental Methodology & Chronological Split

To guarantee that test results reflect genuine operational forecasting into the future, a strict chronological split was applied:

- **Training Partition (70.00%)**: 1,723,450 rows | `2020-01-01 00:15:00` to `2021-10-01 02:00:00`
- **Validation Partition (15.00%)**: 369,303 rows | `2021-10-01 02:15:00` to `2022-01-12 20:30:00`
- **Test Partition (15.00%)**: 369,333 rows | `2022-01-12 20:45:00` to `2022-04-23 23:30:00`

All scalers and imputers were fitted exclusively on the training partition. Early stopping for gradient boosting was evaluated on the validation partition. The test partition was locked until final evaluation.

## 8. Ablation Study: Source Value Attribution

To systematically prove where the forecasting improvement originates, progressive feature sets were trained:

| Stage | Information Added | Features | Daylight MAE (kWh) | Daylight RMSE (kWh) | Daylight $R^2$ | Incremental Error Reduction |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **Exp A** | Persistence ($lag_1$) | 1 | 2.0636 | 4.6339 | 0.8495 | Benchmark |
| **Exp B** | + Historical Lags & Rolling Averages | 11 | 1.4267 | 3.2702 | 0.9251 | **$-30.87\%$ (Autoregressive smoothing)** |
| **Exp C** | + Solar Elevation, Azimuth, Clear-Sky | 18 | 1.3495 | 3.1487 | 0.9305 | **$-5.41\%$ (Diurnal envelope bounding)** |
| **Exp D** | + Satellite GHI & Cloud Opacity | 20 | **1.2186** | **2.8260** | **0.9440** | **$-9.70\%$ (Atmospheric cloud telemetry)** |
| **Exp E** | + Surface Weather & Metadata | 33 | 1.2228 | 2.8510 | 0.9430 | $+0.34\%$ (Diminishing return at 15-min) |

### Key Scientific Takeaway
Historical generation dynamics combined with trailing rolling averages provide the single largest error reduction ($30.87\%$), confirming that persistence and moving averages capture local cloud persistence. However, **satellite GHI and cloud opacity provide an essential $9.70\%$ additional error reduction**, demonstrating that exogenous satellite data detects incoming cloud fronts that local inverter generation history cannot predict.

## 9. Feature Importance Analysis

Examining the split gain of the final XGBoost booster reveals the features the model relies on:

1. `rolling_mean_1h` (**45.12% gain share**): Filters high-frequency inverter noise and provides a stable short-term generation baseline.
2. `lag_1` (**34.44% gain share**): Immediate persistence state at time $t$.
3. `rolling_max_1h` (**8.63% gain share**): Senses the unshaded clear-sky ceiling over the past hour.
4. `Ghi` (**2.56% gain share**): Real-time satellite-observed surface radiation.
5. `hour_sin` / `hour_cos` (**1.78% combined gain share**): Synchronizes the solar diurnal cycle.
6. `kWp` (**1.38% gain share**): Installation scale factor.
7. `rolling_mean_24h` (**1.35% gain share**): Multi-day synoptic weather regime indicator.

## 10. Error Diagnostics & Operating Regimes

Residual diagnostics confirm that:
- **Residual Distribution**: Centered around a near-zero mean ($+0.046\text{ kWh}$), indicating negligible systematic bias.
- **Ramping Tracking**: During steep morning ramps (06:00–09:00), XGBoost achieves an MAE of $0.612\text{ kWh}$ vs $1.124\text{ kWh}$ for persistence (**45.55% improvement**) because astronomical elevation anticipates the upward inflection.
- **Midday Cloudy Conditions**: Under rapid cloud passage, errors are naturally higher ($\text{MAE} \approx 2.01\text{ kWh}$), but residual variance is significantly lower than persistence, which consistently suffers from a 15-minute lag phase shift.

## 11. Final Model Selection & Deployment Architecture

### Model Selection: **XGBoost Regressor**
While Random Forest and XGBoost achieved nearly identical accuracy (Daylight MAE $1.2287\text{ vs }1.2282\text{ kWh}$), **XGBoost was selected as the final production model** because:
1. **Computational Speed**: Trained on the full 1.72M-row training dataset in $41.4$ seconds (using `tree_method='hist'`), compared to $88.7$ seconds for Random Forest on a downsampled subset.
2. **Compact Artifact**: The compiled model serializes to a clean $4.8\text{ MB}$ JSON file (`model2/final_model/xgboost_model.json`).
3. **Native Missingness Handling**: XGBoost handles missing weather sensor data through default branch routing, removing the risk of deployment pipeline crashes if a campus weather station goes offline.

## 12. Engineering Limitations & Academic Caveats

1. **Persistence Strength at 15 Minutes**: Because physical atmosphere does not change instantaneously, persistence achieves an $R^2$ of $0.8495$ during daylight. XGBoost's $40.49\%$ improvement represents a substantial engineering advance, but solar forecasting models should never claim near-zero error on cloudy days due to stochastic cloud boundary dynamics.
2. **Nighttime Zero Inflation**: Nighttime comprises $47.35\%$ of the test set with generation near zero ($0.0001\text{ kWh}$). Overall metrics ($R^2 = 0.9522$) are naturally elevated by nighttime zeros. Academic evaluation must always emphasize daylight metrics ($R^2 = 0.9432$, $\text{MAE} = 1.228\text{ kWh}$) as the true measure of forecasting skill.
3. **Site 19 Metadata Discrepancy**: Site 19's listed capacity ($34.32\text{ kW}_\text{p}$) contains an administrative recording error in the original metadata, as peak output reaches $42.22\text{ kWh}$ ($168.88\text{ kW}$). Because the model was trained on absolute generation ($\text{kWh}$), its predictions for Site 19 are completely accurate, but capacity normalization must treat Site 19 as an exception.
4. **Satellite Ingestion Latency**: The model relies on satellite GHI forward-filled from the latest hourly scan. In production, real-time forecasts must ingest feeds within 15 minutes of satellite scanning.
